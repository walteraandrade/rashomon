# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Journalists, researchers and politically engaged readers in Brazil. They open the atlas to see which words stick to a named public figure in a chosen window of posts, news and speeches. They are not here to configure a dashboard; they are here to read a recorte and check it against the texts.

## Product Purpose

Show, honestly, which words keep landing next to a tracked name, how surprising that is, which outlet said it, and in what tone the model scored it. Success is a reader who can answer "what is being said about this person, by whom, and how unusually?" without leaving the graph they are looking at.

## Brand Personality

Forensic, editorial, dry. Type does the ranking; colour does the meaning. No theatre, no "insights", no leaving the page to be taught.

## Anti-references

SaaS analytics shells. Help centres that dump the reader onto another URL. Walls of instructional prose next to a chart. Rainbow dashboards. Cream-paper editorial templates. Borders used as furniture. Headings that need a coloured accent to be found.

## Design Principles

- Teach next to the mark. A graph carries its own key; a separate page is a shareable copy, never the only door.
- Type ranks. Colour means. Space is the rule line. Nothing else draws a limit.
- One sentence is the recorte. Controls live in that sentence, not in a toolbar of mystery chips.
- Cut copy until a fact remains. If a paragraph restates the key, drop the paragraph.
- Honesty over smoothness. Tone is GDELT-only. Testimony is not comparable across people. Rare words do not dominate.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA for body text. Keyboard reaches every mark the pointer does. Focus is a wash, never a ring, because the site draws no borders. `prefers-reduced-motion` kills the card animation and the mark fades. Colour never carries a fact alone: the key names tamanho, cor, posição in words, and the inspector repeats the numbers.
